Shrink Signal

Eight Eurostat offence categories across 41 countries, turned into a priority order you can defend in a room. It measures recorded crime pressure on a market — not retail shrink, and not loss. That distinction is the whole point, so it is stated here rather than buried.

Read this before the charts

    Loss Pressure Index

    # Country Index Pressure 5-year trend Confidence Categories

    Pressure is the country's percentile against the other reporting countries in the reference year, averaged over the categories it actually reports. Trend is the median change across those categories over the window. Confidence is reporting completeness, not accuracy — a grade A country can still record crime differently from its neighbour.

    Germany, category by category

    Within one country the recording practice is broadly constant, which makes the trend the sounder reading. Each line is indexed to its own first available year, so categories of very different size can share an axis.

    Germany, from the national statistic

    Everything above this line is Eurostat. Everything below it is the Bundeskriminalamt's own Polizeiliche Kriminalstatistik, which counts to German recording rules rather than the harmonised international definition. The two are never joined: splicing a BKA year onto a Eurostat line would put a definition change in the middle of a trend and hide it. What BKA gives that Eurostat cannot is on the right — a shoplifting category, a clearance rate, sixteen Bundesländer, and a year Eurostat has not published yet.
